Every expression you write has a hidden shape. The symbols sit in a row on the page, but the meaning is a tree: one operation on top, whose inputs are themselves expressions.

Definition 1.1 (syntax tree). The syntax tree of an expression has the expression's last operation at its root, and the syntax trees of that operation's inputs as its children.

Try -x^2 versus (-x)^2, or a/b/c, or sin x^2.

Substitution

To substitute an expression for a variable, you replace every occurrence of the variable by a copy of the expression. In terms of syntax trees, each leaf labelled with the variable gets the whole tree of the new expression grafted on in its place. Substituting the letters, rather than the tree, is a common source of mistakes.
